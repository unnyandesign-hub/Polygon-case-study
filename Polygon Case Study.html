Research 01 · Discovery

From clinics to bedrooms.

After 2020, routines moved home and discovery moved to feeds. I mapped how people get from a post to a purchase, and where they drop out.

Finding

People buy on emotion and social proof, then judge on results. Proof has to arrive after the purchase.

The adoption journey · tap a step

What drives adoption +
  • Home spa trend: people trained to do treatments themselves.
  • Cost: one device against recurring salon visits.
  • Privacy and convenience: treatment on your own schedule.
  • Access: professional grade technology adapted for the home.
Research 02 · Market

Which tool, which gap.

I compared device types by efficacy, then placed six leading products by price and innovation and read their reviews. One space stayed empty.

Finding

LED masks work best, but the market splits into premium and uncomfortable or cheap and generic. Comfort plus guidance is open.

Technology trends I tracked +
  • AI personalisation. Skin analysis that adapts treatment to the person.
  • Multi functional devices. Modular tools that do more than one job.
  • Sustainability. Repairable design, trade in, fewer disposable parts.
  • Smart connectivity. App tracking, progress metrics and reminders.
Clinical numbers behind it +
  • 20–30% typical improvement reported for LED therapy.
  • 5–8 weeks before results are visible.
  • 630 nm is the reference red light wavelength.
  • $3.2B LED market in 2024, growing 28% a year.

Research 03 · Analysis

The consistency paradox.

People want these devices and spend real money on them. Then the routine asks for weeks of faith with nothing to show.

Finding

The problem is retention, not hardware. Polygon has to hold people from week 2 to week 6.

Why people drop off +
  • No visible results within 2 to 4 weeks.
  • Storage and setup are a hassle.
  • No habit loop or reminders.
  • Battery rules make travel hard.
  • Manual tools are easy to forget.

Research 05 · User interviews

What users and a dermatologist told me.

Long term and lapsed users in the Netherlands, the UAE and India, checked against an expert view.

    Finding

    Even motivated users fail without guidance on dose, fit and progress. The expert sees guidance as a safety need, not a feature.

    Research 06 · Synthesis

    Why, what, how, what if.

    I clustered everything into four questions, from the problem to solutions worth testing. Open each to see the cluster.

    Design brief

    What if home devices knew your skin?

    A device and app that give people clarity, control and comfort, closing the gap between dermatology and daily life.
    01Why · the core problems+
    • Poor efficacy, discomfort and slow results drive people away.
    • Influencer hype without science breeds mistrust.
    • Devices ignore skin type and how skin changes day to day.
    02What · the needs underneath+
    • Confidence it works for my skin and my concern.
    • Measurable progress, not vague glow claims.
    • Fits my schedule, with little setup.
    • Honesty about what it does and how long it takes.
    03How · design opportunities+
    • AI diagnostics that set the light dose per zone.
    • Light, flexible fit with no hard straps or nose pressure.
    • App guidance with before and after tracking.
    • Hardware without a separate battery pack.
    • Real user feedback in place of influencer claims.
    04What if · solutions to test+
    • A smart mask with zone based calibration.
    • A sensor that reads facial topography over time.
    • Auto scheduling, reminders and dermatology modelled plans.
    • Voice guidance for first time users.
    • An induction charging dock.
    Decision 01

    Treat by zone, from a scan.

    One light for the whole face can’t answer “is this working for me?”. I split the mask into facets so each zone gets its own light, set by a face scan.

    What I considered

    There were a few ways to go. Here’s where I landed, and why.

    I could have…Kept a rigid strap maskBut a hard shell leaves pressure marks and can’t follow different faces.
    I could have…Used even rows of LEDsBut one light everywhere means no calibration and no answer per zone.
    My pickA faceted stretch shellPanels flex on their own, so it fits every face, and each facet is a zone the scan can tune.
    Polygon · 3/4 view, lit by zoneHover a number to see why
    For all face structures
    Decision 02

    Scan, treat, track: one loop.

    Retention was the problem, so I designed the habit before the screens. Every session ends with proof, and the proof makes the next session better.

    What I considered

    I could have…Built a remote control for the maskBut settings alone don’t keep anyone past week 3.
    I could have…Added streaks and badgesBut rewards without results felt like more hype.
    My pickA loop that ends in visible progressCue, routine, reward, investment, with skin data as the reward.

    Outcome

    A concept, ready to present.

    There are no launch numbers, because Polygon hasn’t launched. These are the numbers it was designed against.

    Concept project. Figures come from the research, not from users of Polygon.

    What I’d take forward +
    • An open brief needs a plan first. Writing my own research plan on day one kept ten workstreams from drifting.
    • Retention is a design problem. The devices work well enough. People quit because the ritual gives nothing back.
    • Cut what the sources can’t carry. Lowering the efficacy claim made the case stronger.
